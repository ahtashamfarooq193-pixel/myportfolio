# Editorial workflow

Audience: international small-business owners and prospective development clients. Public articles must be in English. The homepage should not gain blog cards; the public Insights hub is linked from the footer.

Before writing or publishing, read `registry.json`. This is the durable content inventory and internal-link plan; do not rely on chat memory.

Writing style: answer the question immediately, use concrete scenarios and clear headings, explain limitations, and include relevant service links. The user's teacher-provided TubeFetcher article is a structural reference, not text to copy or evidence for our claims.

Search volume is unknown unless a dated, country-specific tool export supports it. Search results demonstrate competing content, not monthly volume. Evaluate relevance, intent and competition as well as volume. Never promise rankings.

Drafts live in `drafts/`. They are not published articles. Only link to published destinations in public copy. Planned article links stay in the registry until the target exists. Avoid creating multiple articles that answer the same primary intent.

For every publication: review factual claims and sources; verify author attribution; build an accessible public article page; add real publication dates, canonical and social metadata, Article/Breadcrumb structured data and a sitemap entry; update registry status; add contextual links from relevant existing pages and link back to the Insights hub. Do not treat Markdown drafts as a substitute for public readable pages.

Images should explain the decision, not decorate filler. Use an original comparison cover and keep comparison tables as accessible HTML. Store only the optimized production asset when a larger source export is not required.

`blog-tracker.csv` is the quick editorial sheet for past and planned articles. Update it whenever a post is drafted, published, redirected or linked from another article. `registry.json` remains the detailed internal-link map used by the build workflow.

Performance follow-up: record Search Console impressions, queries, clicks and dates after publication. Investigate indexing problems separately from content strategy.
